The setup, six days after the print

Q3 FY26 release & call, 2 Sep 2026
Stocklake · Alpha Vantage · Stocktwits · sell-side notes

Broadcom beat, raised every AI number it has, put a $230 billion figure on fiscal 2028 — and the stock fell. Then, over three sessions, it clawed most of it back. The report below is the 25 August teardown re-cut around what the print actually settled and what it left open.

The quarter itself was not close. Revenue of $29.59B (+86%) against a ~$29.4B guide; AI semiconductor revenue $16.7B (+221%) against a $16.0B guide; non-GAAP EPS $3.32 against $3.24 consensus; non-GAAP operating margin a record 67.9%; free cash flow $13.7B, 46% of revenue. Broadcom repaid $5.6B of debt in the quarter and ended with $24.0B of cash.

The guidance did the thing the June print refused to do. FY2026 AI revenue was raised from $56B to $58B. The FY2027 "in excess of $100 billion" became a specific ~$115 billion. And Hock Tan added a year: "line of sight" to $230 billion in FY2028 — "$350 billion of AI semiconductors to these customers in the next two years," with supply "secured" for both. Anthropic alone is now 1 GW in 2026, 5 GW in 2027 and 10 GW in 2028; OpenAI is 1.3 GW in 2027 and "over 5 GW" in 2028; Meta 3 GW through 2028.

Why it sold off anyway. Three things. The Q4 revenue guide of ~$34.8B sat a hair under the $35.0B consensus and the $21.7B AI guide "ticked up but came in slightly below" the street. Infrastructure software printed +29% against its own +31% guide — a ~$150M miss with no explanation offered, and a Q4 guide decelerating to +25%. And when Melius asked for the maximum off-balance-sheet exposure to the XPV financing vehicles, the CFO's answer was "I cannot give you an overarching look at what is the max." That is the sentence the credit market heard.

Then it recovered. After a $342 intraday low on 3 September the stock closed −2.7%, went flat on the 4th, and is +3.2% today at $369 — back on top of the 200-day. Nine houses raised targets, Macquarie upgraded, and three cut (Truist, TD Cowen, DA Davidson to a street-low $350). Consensus FY27 EPS moved down 1% on the print, not up.

What is left is a cleaner argument than the one in August. The demand and the supply are no longer the question — management put numbers on both. The question is now entirely about who pays for the 20 gigawatts and what Broadcom is on the hook for if the two labs cannot.

What the print settled FY27 AI is ~$115B, not ">$100B" — the number the street was already at. FY28 $230B is new. FY26 AI raised to $58B. Q3 AI beat by $0.7B. Gross margin held at 75.0% vs a ~74% guide. Interest-bearing debt is down to $59.4B with a disclosed 4.0% weighted coupon.
What the print left open Software missed its own guide and decelerates again in Q4 (+25%). No backlog, bookings or book-to-bill figure was disclosed for a second straight quarter. No maximum XPV exposure was given — only "modest residual value guarantees… contingent liabilities we view as low risk." The reported >$60B second SPV was neither confirmed nor denied.
The number the market is now arguing about Bernstein's Rasgon, on the call, worked Tan's gigawatts back to ~$11–12B per GW. Tan's answer: "$20 billion–$30 billion… just accept the fact that there is going to be a lot more gigawatts." Whether FY28 is $230B or something closer to half that depends on which of those two numbers is right.

The line this report said to watch — and what it did In August this note flagged receivables at $16.7B and DSO at 69 days, more than double FY22, as the number that would decide whether the working-capital build was a growth build or a collection problem. Q3 answered it: receivables fell to $13.7B on a quarter where revenue rose 33% sequentially, taking DSO to 42 days, and operating cash flow of $14.2B converted 48% of revenue. That is the growth-build reading, confirmed with cash. Inventory is the one line still climbing ($4.5B, +99% vs FY25 year-end), which is what a supply-secured ramp into $21.7B of Q4 AI shipments should look like. Keep watching it; stop worrying about receivables.
What the deleveraging does and doesn't tell you Net debt of $35.4B against $30.5B of trailing free cash flow is comfortable by any measure, and Q3's $5.6B paydown makes it more so. But it measures on-balance-sheet debt only. The Apollo/Blackstone XPV platform sits outside it, and on the Q3 call the CFO confirmed Broadcom "may provide modest residual value guarantees, which are contingent liabilities we view as low risk" — while declining to size the maximum. S&P treats that support as a contingent, debt-like obligation added to adjusted debt. The reported leverage ratio and the rating-agency leverage ratio are still diverging; the Q3 10-Q's guarantee footnote is the next disclosure that narrows it.

Why the agencies moved Moody's cited debt/EBITDA falling below 1.5× from ~3× at FY2024, FCF going $17B → $29B, and $10.7B cash plus a $7.5B revolver. S&P cited AI at 57% of semiconductor sales in Q3 FY25 versus 41% in FY24, leverage 2.0× → 1.2× → ~0.8×, and said plainly that it had been "too conservative over the past two years."
Where the cost-of-debt story stops being reassuring Everything above describes an issuer whose access is improving — and Q3 improved it further, with $5.6B repaid and no new issuance. The two things that still do not appear anywhere in it are the Apollo/Blackstone XPV platform ($35B first tranche closed in June for Anthropic's 1 GW; >20 GW targeted through 2028) and the >$60B second SPV reported in August. The 2 September call was asked directly. What it gave: the platform lets "sophisticated third-party financial partners… independently underwrite and capitalize the assets rather than providing the direct financing ourselves," and "where necessary, we may provide modest residual value guarantees, which are contingent liabilities we view as low risk." What it withheld, when Melius asked for the maximum: "I cannot give you an overarching look at what is the max… we are going to evaluate any strategic financing really on a deal-by-deal basis." Asked whether the 10 GW Anthropic and 5 GW OpenAI 2028 builds would all be XPV-financed, Tan: "Anthropic is well on its way to an IPO… its investment credit will change. OpenAI… it's different." BofA's credit desk had already moved Broadcom's bonds to Marketweight on 11 August, citing a $29B maximum loss exposure on the first tranche and up to $370B of XPV senior debt by mid-2029. Neither vehicle will ever produce a Broadcom pricing term sheet — the FWP history stops at 6 January 2026 — so the Q3 10-Q's guarantee footnote is the only forthcoming primary disclosure. Reported cost of debt: best since before VMware. Market-implied cost of credit: still the worst of the cycle, and the call did not close the gap.

The competitive set

Stocklake batch fundamentals, 25 Aug 2026
Company filings · Bigdata.com

Broadcom competes in three distinct arenas with three different competitor sets — and only one of them is Marvell. Conflating them is the most common analytical error on this name.

  • Custom AI silicon (XPU/ASIC). Direct rivals: Marvell, Alchip, GUC, MediaTek, and increasingly Astera Labs at the connectivity layer. The customer set is six hyperscalers and frontier labs. Broadcom is the incumbent; the 19 August Marvell–Google warrant is the first crack.
  • AI networking (Ethernet switching, SerDes, optics, retimers). Rivals: Arista (systems), Nvidia (InfiniBand/Spectrum-X), Cisco, Credo and Astera Labs at the component layer. Tomahawk 6 at 102.4 Tb/s is the anchor product and was ~40% of Q2 AI revenue.
  • Merchant GPUs. Broadcom does not compete here. Nvidia and AMD are the substitutes for Broadcom's customers' custom chips, not for Broadcom's products — the competitive question is whether hyperscalers keep building their own, not whether AVGO out-designs Nvidia.
  • Infrastructure software. Rivals: Nutanix, Red Hat OpenShift Virtualization, Microsoft Azure Local, Proxmox, and the public clouds. See the VMware section.

Marvell: the comparison everyone asks for

On 19 August, Google granted Marvell a warrant tied to roughly $120 billion of qualifying purchases through FY2033 for custom AI inference chips. AVGO fell 4.6% and shed ~$87B of market cap on the day. On 25 August MRVL rose 5.7% into its 27 August earnings.

Why it matters: Google was Broadcom's founding XPU customer and the TPU franchise is the single largest contributor to the FY27 guide. Coverage also points to MediaTek as another Google partner and rumours of AMD involvement in future TPU generations. Google is deliberately dual-sourcing.

Why it may matter less than the tape implied: JPMorgan pushed back on 20 August, noting Google's 3nm TPU v8i has started ramping with Broadcom and 2nm TPU v9 is expected early 2028 — the Marvell content is a later-generation, incremental award, not a displacement. And the scale gap is enormous: Marvell's entire TTM revenue is $8.7B against Broadcom's $89.1B, at 14.5% operating margin versus 54%.

What the Q3 call added: Marvell was not mentioned once. The Google second source Broadcom chose to name was MediaTek — Tan: "Broadcom is now shipping the TPU v8i ahead of the MediaTek v8t, which in fact was initiated earlier." That is the competitive claim management wants on the record: the incumbent is out-executing the challenger on Google's own roadmap. Arya's direct question — why isn't Google taking more? — was not directly answered.

The competitive question that actually decides the thesis Not "can Marvell take share." It is whether the custom-silicon share of AI compute keeps rising against merchant GPUs at all. If it does, Broadcom's scale, packaging relationships and Ethernet franchise make it the structural winner regardless of a Google second source. If hyperscalers conclude that Nvidia's software stack is worth the margin, Broadcom's six-customer concentration becomes the problem rather than the moat.

Jalapeño — the OpenAI program, and what SemiAnalysis actually argued

SemiAnalysis 25 Aug 2026 (free portion; §8 paywalled)
OpenAI & Broadcom releases · Q3 FY26 call, 2 Sep · The Information · CNBC

SemiAnalysis published "OpenAI' Jalapeño: Better Than Nvidia Blackwell" on 25 August; a week later Broadcom said on its Q3 call that the chip had shipped. The headline is the least interesting part of the SemiAnalysis piece. The argued claim — and the caveats they put on their own work — matter far more to the Broadcom thesis.

What Jalapeño is

OpenAI's first custom inference ASIC, co-developed with Broadcom, unveiled 24 June 2026, benchmarked publicly for the first time on 25 August, and confirmed as shipped on the 2 September call. Silicon specs come almost entirely from SemiAnalysis — OpenAI and Broadcom disclosed essentially none of them.

A reticle-sized ~840 mm² compute die on TSMC N3P with an N3E I/O chiplet and six HBM4 stacks at 15.4 TB/s, CoWoS-packaged, taped out November 2025. A0 silicon does 10.7 PFLOPS MXFP4 at 700 W; B0, in fab, does 13.4 PFLOPS with ~25% better perf-per-watt. For reference, Nvidia's Vera Rubin is 17.5 PFLOPS dense NVFP4 at 900–1,150 W.

Broadcom's dollar content is the network, and it is Ethernet. 128 XPUs per rack meshed all-to-all through six Tomahawk 6 ASICs at 102.4 Tb/s each, giving 4.8 Tb/s per XPU locally; up to 2,048 XPUs in a single scale-up domain over an 8-rail architecture with optical circuit switches and 1.6T transceivers. The October 2025 release said the racks would be "scaled entirely with Ethernet and other connectivity solutions from Broadcom." Celestica does board and rack integration; Microsoft was named a deployment partner in June.

Software is entirely OpenAI's: kernels in "Gluon" (built on Triton), a serving engine called Teacup, with Codex used internally for compiler work.

SemiAnalysis's argument, in their words

The contrarian claims

  • Against the press narrative that this is a narrow OpenAI-model-specific part — which they blame on "throwaway comments from OpenAI" — they argue "OpenAI made a generalized chip for AI inference… capable of running all sorts of models, and all sorts of workloads."
  • "In general first generation chips are not competitive" — yet they assess Jalapeño as "industry leading and beating every Nvidia, AMD, and Google chip."

  • On the gap being software, not silicon: "We don't think that Nvidia hardware is inferior, but more so that Jalapeño's software bring-up has progressed more quickly."   • The line that will get quoted back at Nvidia: "OpenAI models like GPT 5.6 Sol…have been used to design a chip that poses a real threat to the CUDA moat."

The caveats they put on themselves

  • "All numbers are provided to us by OpenAI. We verified the InferenceX runs in person in the lab, but we did not run the full suite… nor have we seen AgentX results." InferenceX is SemiAnalysis's own benchmark and OpenAI invited them.
  • "Comparison to Blackwell is somewhat incomplete and unfair. Jalapeño is really competing against chips like Rubin that also use HBM4."
  • On TCO against the right comparator: "Vera Rubin and Jalapeño are head-to-head, producing almost the same number of output tokens per $" — Jalapeño without speculative decoding, so with it implemented "will serve much lower cost tokens." Read carefully, that is a tie today with optionality, not a rout.
  • They refuse the lazy margin-arbitrage explanation: part of the TCO gain "comes from trading Nvidia's high margins for Broadcom's lower (though still high) margins" — but "not all of it."
  • Vera Rubin systems "are starting to ship to customers right now, while it will still be some time before OpenAI has anything beyond engineering samples."
  • Software gaps persist: "Surprisingly, OpenAI had no internal implementation of MLA kernels until they benchmarked DeepSeek with InferenceX."

The hurdles are, in their words, "mostly hardware: how much can they produce, how well can they deploy and operate datacenters" — because "software is already proven." Whatever else the article says, that sentence is the honest scale of where the OpenAI program sits in August 2026.

Get the customer attribution right first. A widely repeated conflation: the ">$10 billion of orders" from a mystery fourth XPU customer disclosed 5 September 2025 was not OpenAI. Broadcom's semiconductor president said so explicitly on 13 October 2025, and CNBC revealed on 11 December that it was Anthropic. That relationship has since been reported at ~$21B.

The math on OpenAI specifically. Bernstein's Rasgon uses ~$20B of Broadcom revenue per gigawatt and Hock Tan has said the real number is "not far from" that. Regulatory conditions — narrower than commonly assumed

European Commission (M.10806), cleared 12 Jul 2023 with commitments. The Commission narrowed to a single theory of harm: degradation of interoperability between VMware and Marvell's Fibre Channel HBAs, Broadcom's only FC HBA rival. Three limbs — (1) interoperability access to VMware APIs, dev kits, pre-release materials and certification on terms equal to Broadcom's own FC HBA division; (2) a source-code grant of Broadcom FC HBA drivers to Marvell to reuse and modify; (3) organisational firewall between the FC HBA team and VMware certification. Duration: 10 years to ~Nov 2033, with a monitoring trustee and fast-track dispute resolution. The Commission explicitly rejected concerns on storage adapters, NICs, SmartNICs and bundling.

UK CMA — cleared unconditionally, 21 Aug 2023, after a full Phase 2. Found no substantial lessening of competition. No remedies at all — the notable divergence from the EU and China.

China SAMR — conditional approval 21 Nov 2023 after a record 231-day pre-review. Six conditions imposed, four published and two kept confidential. The published four: (1) no tying or bundling of Broadcom FC adapters, storage adapters and Ethernet NICs with VMware in the Chinese market; (2) continued interoperability with third-party hardware; (3) continued driver development and certification for third-party virtualisation software; (4) confidential-information protection for third-party hardware makers. 10 years, auto-lapsing. China is the only jurisdiction that imposed an explicit anti-tying obligation. Confidence note: the four conditions are corroborated across Chinese media and an ABA antitrust analysis; the 10-year duration and "six, two secret" structure rest on Chinese-language reporting, not a retrieved English primary text.

The gap the remedies leave open None of the EU, CMA or SAMR conditions constrain the thing customers actually object to — software pricing, SKU bundling, or channel access. That is precisely the gap CISPE's Article 102 complaint (19 Mar 2026, with a request for interim measures) is trying to close. CISPE has also sued in the EU General Court (Jul 2025) to annul the clearance decision itself. In May 2026 the General Court rejected Broadcom's attempt to suspend a Commission RFI on US in-house-privilege grounds. No formal Article 101/102 proceedings have been opened — treat "formal EU investigation" claims as imprecise.

What VMware is now

VMware Cloud Foundation (VCF) is effectively the only full-stack SKU. VCF 9.0 shipped 17 Jun 2025; 9.1 on 5 May 2026. It bundles vSphere (compute), vSAN (storage, 1 TiB/core), NSX (networking and security), vSphere Kubernetes Service, VCF Operations and Automation (the former Aria), HCX, and VCF Private AI services. An "Advanced Services" add-on layer — cyber compliance, security, Avi load balancing, data services, network observability — is the current up-sell vector and a live customer grievance.

VMware Private AI Foundation with NVIDIA is the answer to the obvious bear case that AI workloads bypass the hypervisor. The VCF-side package is included in VCF; NVIDIA AI Enterprise licences are bought separately from NVIDIA. 9.0/9.1 added air-gapped deployment, multi-tenancy, GPU/vGPU monitoring, FIPS/STIG containers for government, and ConnectX-7 / BlueField-3 / Blackwell support. Litigation and regulatory sequel

  • AT&T v. Broadcom (NY, filed 29 Aug 2024) — ~75,000 VMs, 22,000 supporting public-safety agencies; AT&T alleged a "+1,050% in one year" proposed increase. TRO granted; settled in principle 13 Dec 2024, terms undisclosed.
  • Broadcom/VMware v. Siemens AG (D. Del., 21 Mar 2025) — Broadcom suing the customer over ~23,000 alleged unlicensed US deployments. Ongoing, counterclaims pending.
  • Rijkswaterstaat (Dutch Ministry of Infrastructure) — Broadcom lost. 1 Jul 2025, The Hague found it "acts in breach of its duty of care," ordered up to two years of exit support at €250k/day penalty capped at €25M. Trigger was an 85% increase on systems running tunnels, locks and bridges.
  • Tesco plc (UK High Court, Sep 2025) — ≥£100M claim, ~40,000 workloads. Alleges 175% VMware and 350% mainframe increases. Trial window Nov 2027 – Feb 2028; Tesco migrating off by end-2027.
  • Frequently mis-cited: the UnitedHealth suit (Apr 2025) is about CA mainframe software, not VMware.
The structural read The gap between "86–87% reducing" and "4% fully migrated" is the entire VMware story. Broadcom is monetising an installed base that is shrinking in seat count while paying far more per seat. Q3 showed both halves at once: record revenue and 94% gross margin, alongside a guide miss, decelerating ARR, and a Q4 that goes sideways. Whether revenue keeps growing depends on price/mix uplift outrunning core-count attrition — and that race resolves in the FY2027 renewal cohort, when the three-year subscriptions signed in the late-2023/2024 conversion wave come up from November–December 2026 onward. Management's own product answer is "VMware Private AI Cloud" and public-cloud repatriation; it gave no adoption metric for either.

Strengths

  • The only credible full-stack custom-AI vendor. Compute ASIC design, Ethernet switching at 102.4 Tb/s, SerDes, optics, PCIe and rack-scale deployment under one roof. Competitors have pieces; Broadcom has the whole path from die to rack.
  • Margin structure that funds everything. 67.9% non-GAAP operating margin in Q3 FY26 — a record, up 240bps YoY — with 94%-gross-margin software subsidising the semiconductor build-out. Gross margin held at 75% against a 74% guide. Capex under 2% of revenue.
  • Cash generation. $30.5B TTM free cash flow; Q3's $13.7B was 46% of revenue. $5.6B of debt repaid in a quarter with no buybacks. Fifteen consecutive dividend increases.
  • Improving cost of capital. BBB− at the VMware announcement to A−/A3 by September 2025; the 5-year new-issue spread compressed +85bp → +53bp; interest expense is down 4% year over year on flat debt as ~7% floating term loans were refinanced into 4.2–5.2% fixed notes.
  • Visibility, now with numbers. FY27 AI "approximately $115B" and FY28 "line of sight" to $230B, both with "secured the supply" attached — $350B over two years. Management says demand exceeds even that outlook.
  • Customer lock-in through co-design. Multi-generation roadmaps with Google (through 2031), Meta (through 2028) and OpenAI (through 2029) are architectural commitments, not purchase orders.
  • Execution track record under Tan. VMware opex halved, the $8.5B incremental-EBITDA commitment beaten ahead of a three-year schedule.

Weaknesses

  • Six customers, two of them the growth. The FY27 ~$115B guide averages ~$19B per customer, and management itself separated "the other four [who] are financially secure" from Anthropic and OpenAI — whose 2028 builds (10 GW and >5 GW) carry most of the step to $230B and may be XPV-financed "in some cases."
  • Structural gross-margin dilution. XPU carries ~70% semiconductor gross margin against 94% software; consolidated non-GAAP GM went 77% → 75% and is guided to ~73% in Q4. Tan's answer — "stop focusing on gross margin" — is an argument, not a fix.
  • Infrastructure software deceleration. +1%, +9%, then +29% that missed its own +31% guide, now guided to +25% with ARR growth slowing from 17% to 15%. The ballast has become the swing factor.
  • No balance-sheet support. Roughly two-thirds of assets are intangible; tangible book value per share is about −$5. $59.4B of debt against $24.0B cash before any XPV exposure.
  • Inventory is building. $4.5B, +99% since the FY25 year-end — appropriate for a supply-secured ramp into a $21.7B AI quarter, but the one working-capital line still rising after Q3 collapsed DSO from 69 to 42 days.
  • Customer relations as a liability. Four significant VMware suits, one already lost in the Netherlands, plus an active EU complaint. Reputational cost is now a pricing constraint in Europe.
  • Disclosure quality slipping. For a second straight quarter Broadcom gave no backlog, bookings or book-to-bill figure, and did not disclose adjusted EBITDA at all. It has also declined, when asked directly, to size its maximum XPV exposure.

Opportunities

  • Custom silicon taking share from merchant GPUs. The core structural bet. If ASIC share of AI compute keeps rising, Broadcom is the default beneficiary regardless of which lab wins.
  • Ethernet displacing InfiniBand for scale-up. Jalapeño's 2,048-XPU domain built entirely on Tomahawk 6 is the proof point Broadcom has wanted for three years. Networking was ~40% of Q2 AI revenue.
  • FY2028 is now a number. $230B "line of sight," roughly 20 GW on Bernstein's arithmetic, with Anthropic 10 GW, OpenAI >5 GW and Meta 3 GW disclosed individually. Cantor models FY28 EPS of $35 against a $27 consensus — the stock at 10.6× that.
  • Private AI inference on-prem. VCF Private AI with NVIDIA converts the hypervisor base into an AI attach point; Broadcom claims public-cloud inference usage fell 15 points YoY. If even directionally right, this reframes VMware from melting ice cube to distribution channel.
  • Two undisclosed XPU customers shipping from late 2026 (~$6B in orders) plus whatever the ~$21B Anthropic relationship becomes.
  • Multiple re-rating. At 19.1× FY27 consensus the stock is cheaper than the market on forward earnings, and the Q3 10-Q's guarantee footnote is the next chance for the SPV to prove benign. Macquarie's upgrade thesis: "the cleanest listed way to own the build" ahead of an Anthropic IPO.

Threats

  • The financing vehicle is the dominant risk — and the call did not size it. The $35B first XPV tranche is closed; a >$60B second raise is reported. Broadcom "may provide modest residual value guarantees," and when asked for the maximum: "I cannot give you an overarching look at what is the max." BofA's credit desk models up to $370B of senior debt by mid-2029 with $10.5–42B of Broadcom loss exposure and has the bonds at Marketweight. TD Cowen cut on exactly this: "circular financing."
  • Loss of Google exclusivity. The 19 Aug Marvell warrant covering ~$120B of qualifying purchases through FY2033 breaks the de facto monopoly on TPU. MediaTek is reportedly a second partner and AMD is rumoured for later generations.
  • Customer capex pause. A spending slowdown at any one of OpenAI, Anthropic or Meta materially breaks the FY27 guide. OpenAI's 10 GW commitment is not underwritten by its own cash flows.
  • The FY2027 VMware renewal cliff. Three-year contracts from the 2023–24 conversion wave renew from Nov–Dec 2026 into a market with mature alternatives. Even 10–15% churn among the top ~2,000 accounts weakens the recurring-revenue narrative.
  • EU regulatory action. CISPE's Article 102 complaint with interim-measures request could force unbundling or restored legacy licensing. Broadcom has already lost a privilege fight in the General Court.
  • Supply chain. Reticle-sized N3P dies plus HBM4 plus CoWoS put Broadcom in direct competition with Nvidia, Google and AMD for the same scarce TSMC advanced-packaging and HBM4 capacity.
  • The $/GW arithmetic. Rasgon's reconciliation of the disclosed gigawatts implies ~$11–12B per GW; Tan insists on $20–30B. If the lower figure is right, FY28 is closer to $115B than $230B — and the FY28 number is now the multiple's anchor.
