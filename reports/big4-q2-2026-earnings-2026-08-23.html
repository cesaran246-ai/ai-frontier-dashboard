Executive summary

One quarter, two verdicts: the market paid for visible returns and punished open-ended spend.

GOOG
Cloud rips, cash turns
  • Revenue $119.8B (+24%); Cloud +82% to $24.8B; backlog $514B (+$52B QoQ)
  • Op margin 34.0% (+1.6pp YoY, but −2.1pp QoQ); EPS $9.11, $2.85 ex equity gains
  • Capex 2026 raised again: $195–205B (+$15B both ends)
  • FCF −$5.9B — first negative quarter as a public company
  • Stock −6.9% next day (EU DMA fine same morning)
MSFT
The re-rating quarter
  • Revenue $90.0B (+18%); Azure +43%, fastest since 2022; FY26 Azure > $100B
  • RPO $678B (+84%); +25% ex-OpenAI; all sequential growth from non-frontier customers
  • Calendar-2026 capex guide cut to ~$175B from ~$190B — useful life 15→25 yrs, lease reclass
  • First explicit “FCF positive in FY27” commitment; FQ1 capex still >$50B
  • Stock +15.5% next day — largest one-day cap gain since 2008
META
Charges, capex, no timeline
  • Revenue $60.8B (+28%) but op income −8%; margin 31% (−12pp)
  • $2.4B legal + $1.2B severance (8,000 roles cut in May); ex-items op income +9%
  • FCF $784M vs $12.4B in Q1; capex $31.1B; buybacks $0; $25B bond priced Apr 30
  • FY26 capex $130–145B (floor raised); expenses $165–169B; no 2027 number
  • Stock −8.0%; every published PT was cut; JPM to Neutral
AMZN
AWS inflects, capex breaks
  • Net sales $200.6B (+20%); op income $27.5B (+43%); margin 13.7%
  • AWS +36.7% — 18-quarter high, 5th straight acceleration; margin 39.4%
  • Backlog $496B (+$132B QoQ); AI and chips each >$25B run-rate
  • FY26 capex ~$220B from ~$200B — attributed to memory prices
  • EPS $5.75 incl. $53.4B Anthropic mark; ~$1.92 underlying. Stock +15.3%

The market verdict — same week, opposite reactions

All four grew faster than a year ago. Two stocks were re-rated up 15%, two were sold off 7%.

What got rewarded
A contracted demand signal (MSFT RPO $678B, AWS backlog $496B), capex discipline or at least capex clarity, and an explicit free-cash-flow commitment. Both winners paired accelerating cloud growth with a number investors could hold management to.
What got punished
Open-ended spend without a return timeline. GOOG raised capex a second time in three months into negative FCF; META raised the capex floor, refused a 2027 number, and added a third unquantified revenue pillar. Growth was not the problem — visibility was.

Theme 1 — AI demand vs. compute supply

Every one of the four said demand still exceeds supply. The difference was how much each would promise about returns.

GOOG
  • Pichai: “we continue to be supply constrained, a sign of momentum and rapid adoption”
  • Ashkenazi: “While we have increased our capacity quite significantly over the past three years, the demand still outpaces that investment”
  • Will expand third-party capacity in Q3 as a bridge — “modest margin pressure in the near term”
  • Pichai: “working within a disciplined ROIC framework… the dynamics look healthier than a year ago”
  • Model APIs at ~22B tokens/minute, up from 16B a quarter ago
MSFT
  • Hood: “demand continues to exceed available supply… you can see it in the pricing in the spot market”
  • Nadella: added 31 data centers this quarter (88 this year); “another gigawatt”; on track to double capacity in two years
  • Dock-to-live GPU time down ~50% over the fiscal year; extra in-quarter capacity “quickly monetized”
  • ~2/3 of capex is short-lived assets — Hood: “if the demand environment changes, you just slow down the largest component”
  • Maia 200: 30% better perf/$, now serving both OpenAI and MAI models
META
  • Li: “the industry has historically underbuilt… capacity is going to remain tight for the foreseeable future”
  • Zuckerberg: “we're getting a lot of offers for compute at a significant premium over what we paid for it”
  • New third pillar: enterprise APIs, business agents, “potentially selling compute directly”
  • But: “there's simply not enough compute to meet all demand… short-sighted to sell it all for short-term profits”
  • Nowak (MS) pressed for a quantifiable ROIC timeline — none given
AMZN
  • Jassy: even at ~$220B “we will still not have enough capacity to meet all the demand we have in 2026… also true in 2027”
  • “In fact, the demand we already have for 2028 is striking”
  • Adoption curve is “very barbelled” — AI labs and a few runaway apps at one end, enterprises at the other
  • Expect to have double the power capacity by end-2027 vs 2025
  • Anthropic and OpenAI both hold multi-year, multi-gigawatt Trainium commitments

Theme 2 — Cloud & the contracted backlog

Three of the four now disclose a backlog number, and this quarter that number did more work than the revenue line.

GOOG
  • Cloud $24.8B, +82% — up from +63% in Q1, fifth straight acceleration
  • Cloud op margin 35.6% vs 20.7% a year ago; op income more than tripled to $8.8B
  • Backlog $514B, +$52B QoQ; “just over 50% recognized as revenue over the next 24 months”
  • First quarter recognizing TPU system sales delivered into customer data centers — vast majority of that revenue lands in 2027
  • Customer acquisition velocity more than doubled YoY; existing customers exceeding commitments by >50%
MSFT
  • Microsoft Cloud $59.3B, +27%; Azure +43% vs ~40% consensus; guided to ~45% cc next quarter
  • RPO $678B (+84%), duration 2.3 yrs; ~30% recognized within 12 months (+37% YoY)
  • “All sequential commercial RPO growth was driven by commitments from customers outside of frontier model companies” — Hood
  • Bookings +18% ex-OpenAI (+10% including); FY26 cloud revenue “nearly 90% from non-frontier customers”
  • OpenAI: $24.1B of FY26 revenue, $6.0B receivable, ~25% as-converted stake (10-K)
META
  • Not a cloud provider — but the enterprise/compute pillar is new this quarter
  • Li on funding: “evolving our capital structure to include a greater mix of debt to bring down our cost of capital”
  • BlackRock El Paso JV announced the day before the print: ~$14B, 1 GW, BlackRock 80% / Meta 20%, online 2028
  • ~$13B of residual value guarantees; Squali (Truist) pressed on Blue Owl / Hyperion / BlackRock off-balance-sheet exposure
  • Zuckerberg: “higher margin on selling intelligence rather than selling compute directly”
AMZN
  • AWS $42.2B, +36.7% — fastest in 18 quarters, on a base more than 2× the size
  • Sequential add of +$4.6B — “about 80% more than our largest increase ever”
  • Backlog $496B, +$132B QoQ, “growing triple digits year-over-year”; run-rate $169B
  • Olsavsky: “strong linkage between AI spend and core growth… as customers invest in AI we see a corresponding increase in core consumption”
  • Jassy: AWS could “very possibly be a trillion-dollar annual revenue business for us in time”

Theme 3 — Ad market & the consumer

Advertising was strong everywhere. The divergence was in what it cost to produce that growth.

GOOG
  • Search & other +17%; retail and finance the largest contributors; total ads +14%
  • YouTube ads +13%, helped by the World Cup — Rosenblatt flagged this as partly non-recurring
  • AI Mode past 1B MAU; “driving an incremental increase in search queries overall”
  • Cost per AI Mode response at its lowest since launch on hardware/engineering optimization
  • AI Max out of beta with 500K advertisers; AI-powered campaigns average +15% conversions at similar ROAS
MSFT
  • Search & news ads ex-TAC +10%; LinkedIn +12%, marketing-solutions led
  • More Personal Computing −4%; Windows OEM −5%, Xbox content & services −10%
  • Hood: OEM results ahead of expectations as “partners continue to build inventory given increasing component prices”
  • FY27 guide: Windows OEM & Devices down high teens for the full year
  • Nadella expects Xbox to “return the business to growth in fiscal 2027”
META
  • Ad revenue $59.4B (+27%); impressions +14% (decelerating from +19%), price per ad +12%
  • Pricing was carried by US & Canada (+11%) and Rest of World (+15%) — Europe and APAC only +1% each
  • New Meta Generative Recommender (LLM-based ad retrieval) replaced per-ad scoring; GEM + sequence learning gave +8.3% ad clicks, +15.7% conversions on Facebook
  • Advantage+ end-to-end at a >$75B annual run-rate; 9M SMBs using AI creative tools
  • Zuckerberg: “our ads business is reporting faster year-over-year revenue growth than any other company's reported ad business”
AMZN
  • Advertising $19.8B, +26% — accelerating from +22% in Q1
  • Jassy: shoppers clicking a sponsored prompt convert 48% more often and spend 21% more
  • Alexa for Shopping used by >350M customers in 12 months; active users nearly doubled, interactions +5×
  • Paid units +17%; ~$600M of tariff refunds landed in North America
  • Olsavsky: Amazon “largely absorbed” tariff costs; prices average 14% below other retailers (Profitero)

Theme 4 — Margins, depreciation and the cash line

The P&L still looks fine. The cash flow statement is where this cycle now shows up.

GOOG
  • Op margin 34.0% — up 1.6pp YoY but down 2.1pp sequentially, the first down-quarter of the cycle
  • 1H26 depreciation $13.6B vs $9.5B (+43%); R&D +32%
  • Google Services margin fell to 41.8%, dragged by a $1.5B PriceRunner legal charge
  • FCF −$5.9B (from +$10.1B in Q1) — a $16B swing, partly TPU inventory built ahead of delivery
  • Ashkenazi: “we expect free cash flow will remain under pressure”
MSFT
  • Op margin ~45%, “increased slightly”; gross margin 67%, down YoY on Azure mix and AI infra
  • Useful life of data centers and office buildings extended 15 → 25 years from FY27 — “minimal benefit to FY27 operating income”
  • FCF $19.6B, −23%; capital returned $10.2B in the quarter, >$43B for FY26
  • FY27 guide: op margin down less than 1 point, opex +mid-to-high single digits, FCF positive
  • Headcount 223K, −2% — first annual decline since 2016, product R&D roles −3,000
META
  • Op margin 31% vs 43% — total expenses +55% to $42.0B
  • $2.4B legal charges + $1.18B severance; ex-items, op income would have been +9% (~36.8% margin)
  • Family of Apps op income −6% YoY — the cash-cow segment absorbed AI cost for the first time
  • FCF $784M vs $12.4B in Q1; buybacks $0 for a second quarter; LT debt $83.7B after a $25B six-tranche bond
  • Effective tax rate normalized to 16% after Q1's one-time benefit; guided to 15–17%
AMZN
  • Consolidated margin 13.7% (+2.3pp); AWS margin 39.4%, +650bp YoY (+520bp ex a derivative gain)
  • ~$1.2B of one-time operating benefits: ~$600M tariff refunds (NA) + ~$600M energy-derivative gain (AWS)
  • TTM FCF −$7.6B, from +$1.2B a quarter ago; quarterly cash capex $43.2B → $53.1B
  • Jassy's unit economics: <3 years to break even on servers, 5–6 year server life, 30+ year data centre life, 5–6 server generations per shell
  • “We see the margins and returns in AI tracking what we saw with Core at the same point of evolution, actually a little ahead”

Theme 5 — Forward guidance

Where each management team put its numbers for the next quarter and the next year.

GOOG
  • No formal revenue guide (unchanged practice)
  • FY26 capex $195–205B; 2027 to “increase significantly”, number deferred
  • Q3: slight FX headwind vs a 1pp tailwind in Q2; begins lapping last year's Search acceleration
  • Cloud margin: modest near-term pressure from third-party capacity used as a bridge
  • TPU system revenue: small portion in 2026, “vast majority… realized in 2027”
MSFT
  • FQ1 FY27 revenue $89.85–90.95B (+16–17%)
  • Intelligent Cloud $40.95–41.25B; Azure ~45% cc; PBP $36.7–37.0B; MPC $12.2–12.7B
  • FQ1 capex >$50B; CY26 ~$175B as reported (~$190B like-for-like)
  • FY27: double-digit revenue and operating income growth; margin down <1pt; tax rate ~20%
  • “We expect to remain free cash flow positive in FY27” — Hood
META
  • Q3 revenue $61–64B; ~1% FX headwind (vs a tailwind last quarter)
  • FY26 expenses raised at the floor to $165–169B to absorb the legal charge
  • FY26 capex $130–145B — floor raised, range narrowed from $125–145B
  • Tax rate 15–17% for remaining quarters, up from 13–16%
  • Li: “we aren't providing a specific outlook for 2027 CapEx at this time… visibility declines as you get to '28 and beyond”
AMZN
  • Q3 net sales $197–202B (+9–12%) — below the ~$204B consensus, but ~400bp of that is Prime Day timing
  • Q3 operating income $22.5–26.5B vs $17.4B a year ago (+41% at the midpoint)
  • FX an ~80bp headwind; Prime Day ran June 23–26, inside Q2 this year and entirely in Q3 last year
  • FY26 cash capex ~$220B, up from ~$200B, “the higher cost of memory pushing this number up”
  • No 2027 capex number; capacity for 2027 “largely reserved”, with some of 2028 already reserved

Five takeaways

01

The dispersion is the story, not the spend.

All four are spending more than they earn in cash on AI infrastructure, and all four grew faster than a year ago. But MSFT +15.5% and AMZN +15.3% versus GOOG −6.9% and META −8.0% in the same week says the market has stopped paying for capex as a demand signal and started paying for contracted backlog plus a cash commitment.

02

Microsoft's capex “cut” is an accounting change — but it worked.

The CY26 guide fell $190B → ~$175B purely from extending useful life 15 → 25 years and shifting future leases from finance to operating. Hood said underlying spend expectations are unchanged. Paired with the first explicit “FCF positive in FY27”, it produced the largest one-day market-cap gain since 2008. The capex line is now a definitional break — footnote MSFT in any cross-company series.

03

Free cash flow went negative at both Alphabet and Amazon.

GOOG posted −$5.9B, its first negative FCF quarter as a public company (from +$10.1B in Q1); AMZN's TTM FCF flipped to −$7.6B. Meta's quarterly FCF fell to $784M. Alphabet has stopped buying back stock, raised $49.6B of equity in June (including $10B from Berkshire) and carries $98.2B of debt against ~$16B a year ago. The funding model has changed, not just the spending.

04

Reported EPS is unusable this quarter without adjustment.

GOOG's $9.11 includes a $99.0B unrealized equity gain (mostly SpaceX) worth $6.26 of EPS — $2.85 ex-item. AMZN's $5.75 includes a $53.4B Anthropic mark; underlying is roughly $1.92. Meta's $6.18 is depressed by $3.6B of legal and severance charges. Only Microsoft's number is clean.

05

Combined 2026 capex is now ~$732B guided — ~$747B like-for-like.

Against ~$710B guided a quarter ago and ~$415B actually spent in 2025. Amazon's $20B raise was explicitly attributed to memory prices, not capacity; Microsoft flagged component pricing four times in April and again in July. Cost inflation is now a visible line item inside the AI build, and every 2027 comment — “significantly higher”, “will grow”, “demand for 2028 is striking” — points up.
