Executive summary

  • Growth is real and compounding. Revenue went $22M → $43M → $130M (2023–25) and is guided to $280–290M in 2026 — a 2.2x step every year. RPO of $485M is 1.9x trailing revenue. Q2'26 organic growth was +132%; the rest came from acquisitions closed in 2025.
  • The cost of that growth has scaled faster. Adjusted EBITDA loss widened from $(78)M to $(187)M to a guided $(320)M; R&D is 2.4x revenue and SBC was 2.4x revenue in 2025. The company funded it with ~$3.4B of 2025 equity/warrant financing; share count went 218M → 405M in 18 months (+86%).
  • Technicals are neutral-to-weak. Price is below the 20/50/200-day averages ($42.5/$43.2/$44.6), RSI 50, MACD histogram just negative, DeMark sell-setup active. The chart shows a completed lower high ($72 in May vs $84.6 in Oct) — a break of $32–33 support opens the $26 low; reclaiming $44.6 (SMA200) is the first bullish tell.
  • Ownership is institutional and net-accumulating by count, net-distributing by dollars. 948 institutions hold 61% (573 increased, 275 decreased). Insiders own 0.7%; 12-month Form 4 activity is $9.8M of discretionary/option-exercise sales vs $225K of open-market buys. Short interest halved from 86M (Feb) to 42M shares.
  • Retail is split and exhausted. Stocktwits sentiment score 39 (Bearish) today despite 77% of tagged posts bullish; message volume is "slightly low" — the crowd that rode 2025 is under water and arguing. Sentiment spikes have led price by 1–2 weeks at every turn this year.
  • SkyWater is the pivot from quantum start-up to quantum manufacturer. IonQ paid ~$1.8B for a ~$600M-revenue, ~20%-gross-margin U.S. Trusted Foundry that already fabs chips for D-Wave and PsiQuantum. It makes IonQ the only vertically integrated quantum company — and dilutes its growth rate and margin profile from 2027.

Warrant liability sits in "other non-current liabilities" ($3.16B at Jun-30). Its mark-to-market moves inversely with the stock: the −$1.6B Q2 charge reflects the stock rising into quarter-end. It is non-cash but represents real future dilution if exercised.

Gross margin is now a mix story

FY gross margin: 63% (2023) → 52% (2024) → 40% (2025) → 25% (H1'26). The compression is not pricing — it is hardware system deliveries (KISTI, QuantumBasel, Cambridge) recognized at product margins plus amortization of acquired intangibles in COGS. Cloud/QCaaS and networking/security software carry higher margins; multi-product attach (25% of Q2 revenue) is the lever. Post-SkyWater, consolidated gross margin will trend toward the foundry's ~18–23% until quantum-chip volumes scale.

Short interest, and what the Street is paying for

Chart structure

Two full boom-bust cycles in 12 months: +230% (Aug→Oct '25) then −69%; +170% (Mar→May '26) then −55%. Each rally was catalyst-driven (99.99% fidelity/Oxford close; Q1 beat + 256q sale) and each faded within 6–8 weeks as financing/warrant supply met it. The Aug 5 beat produced only a 51% off-the-low bounce that has now given back half. Pattern: lower high, equal-ish lows — a descending triangle on the weekly, resolving on Sep 8. Bullish invalidation: weekly close above $47.8. Bearish trigger: daily close below $37 with volume > 25M.

08 · SWOT

SWOT analysis

Strengths

  • Only quantum pure play with a nine-figure run-rate: $80M/qtr, 10x the next pure play; five straight record quarters and two guidance raises in 2026.
  • Best physical qubits in the industry: 99.99% two-qubit fidelity (EQC), longest coherence, all-to-all connectivity, lowest QEC overhead; break-even QLDPC demonstrated on hardware.
  • Vertically integrated after SkyWater: design (Oxford Ionics) → fab (200 mm, Trusted Foundry) → packaging (Florida) → systems → cloud. No competitor owns its fab.
  • Balance sheet: ~$2.0B pro-forma cash, negligible debt; ~4 years of runway at current burn.

09 · Competitors & technology (cont.)

How the technologies differ, and where the debate is

How the technologies differ, in one paragraph each

Trapped ions use individual charged atoms (ytterbium/barium) held in electromagnetic traps; every ion is identical by nature, so fidelity is highest and coherence longest, but gates are slow (microseconds) and a single trap holds ~100–200 ions. IonQ's break from Quantinuum is how the ions are controlled: Quantinuum steers them with lasers and shuttles ions through junctions; IonQ patterns microwave antennas on the chip (EQC), which is what lets it tape out chips at a foundry. Superconducting qubits are lithographed circuits cooled to 15 mK; they switch in nanoseconds and use standard fab, but each qubit is slightly different, coherence is short, and error correction needs ~1,000 physical qubits per logical one. Neutral atoms are uncharged atoms held by optical tweezers; they pack densely (1,000+ per array) and can be rearranged mid-computation, sitting between ions and SC on speed and fidelity. Photonic systems encode qubits in light; they run at room temperature and network natively, but gates are probabilistic and loss is the enemy. Annealers (D-Wave) are special-purpose optimizers, not universal computers.

Where the debate actually is

  • Speed vs fidelity: Rigetti's "10,000x faster gates" is true per gate; IonQ's counter is that logical operations per second after error correction favour the modality with 10–100x lower physical error. Kearney-verified time-to-solution results back IonQ on today's NISQ workloads.
  • Scale: neutral atoms and SC show 1,000+ and 150+ qubit devices; ions show ~100. IonQ's answer (256 → 10K on a die via multiplexing, then 2.5D packaging, plus photonic interconnect) is the roadmap the stock is priced on and it has not yet been demonstrated by anyone.
  • Error correction: Quantinuum has the best logical-qubit demos (near five-nines); Google has below-threshold surface codes; D-Wave claims Λ=10 on dual-rail; IonQ has break-even QLDPC on hardware. Logical-qubit count per dollar, not physical count, will decide the 2027–29 winner.
  • Manufacturing: only IonQ (SkyWater) and IBM (Anderon, with $1B DoC) will own a quantum fab. Everyone else rents — often from SkyWater.

What it is

SkyWater (Bloomington, MN; founded 2017 from a Cypress Semiconductor fab) is the largest exclusively U.S.-based pure-play foundry and a DMEA-accredited Category 1A Trusted Foundry — the highest clearance for defense semiconductor work. It runs a "Technology-as-a-Service" model: customers co-fund process development (ATS — Advanced Technology Services), then move to volume Wafer Services. It is deliberately not a leading-edge fab: it works at mature nodes (90–130 nm and above) on specialty processes — superconducting, photonic, rad-hard, MEMS, carbon nanotube, and now trapped-ion chips.

How competitive is it?

  • In quantum, it is the incumbent merchant fab. Named customers: D-Wave (Advantage2 qubits fabricated at SkyWater), PsiQuantum, SQC, QuamCore, plus two confidential programs starting 2026. A decade of superconducting/photonic process modules. IonQ now owns the fab its competitors use.
  • In mainstream foundry, it is small. ~$600M revenue vs GlobalFoundries ~$7B, Tower ~$1.5B, X-Fab ~$1B. It competes on trust (U.S.-only, DMEA 1A), flexibility and co-development, not on scale or cost. Gross margins of 18–23% reflect that.
  • New competition is coming from IBM. The $1B DoC-backed Anderon "first pure-play quantum chip foundry" (May '26) targets exactly SkyWater's quantum niche — but for superconducting. Trusted-foundry status and existing customer processes are SkyWater's moat; IBM's is capital and IP.
  • Customer-funded capex (customers pay for tools) makes it capex-light for a fab — but also means revenue is tied to program funding, as the Q1'26 A&D stop-work showed.
