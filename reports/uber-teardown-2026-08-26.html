Capital allocation what the FCF buys

  • Buyback policy: ~50% of FCF. $3.5B repurchased 1H26; Q2 paused as ~$4B went into Delivery Hero shares. CFO: rebuild "in months, not quarters."
  • AV bootstrap: >$10B committed over the past year across equity stakes (Lucid $500M, WeRide +$100M, Nuro, Pony, Wayve) and fleet off-take (120,000 vehicle commitments). Exploring third-party financing vehicles so it isn't all balance sheet.
  • Monetizing legacy stakes: sold 139.5M Aurora shares in Jun–Aug 2026 (~$950M); retains 186.5M. Q2 equity revaluation gain $1.6B.
  • Leverage: LTD $10.7B, €14B bridge for Delivery Hero; gross leverage capped <2× with investment-grade rating maintained.
  • Dilution: SBC ~$1.9B/yr (3.5% of revenue); diluted share count −4.7% from the FY24 peak.

AV / robotaxi thesis

Uber abandoned in-house autonomy in 2020 and now runs the opposite play: be the demand layer that every AV developer except Tesla plugs into. Management frames the industry like foundation models — many capable suppliers, no single winner — and Uber as the distribution that wins regardless. The bear case is that the two suppliers with the most cars (Waymo, Tesla) don't need Uber.

Bottom line on AV. The stock's 2026 de-rating is the market assigning a real probability to the "Waymo + Tesla don't need you" outcome. The counter-evidence is now dated and specific: seven cities live, roughly ten more launching within twelve months, 120k vehicles committed, and organic trip growth accelerating in the most AV-penetrated metros. If the 15-city target is hit and Q3–Q4 launches show AV rides being fulfilled through Uber at UberX-like pricing, the multiple has room to mean-revert toward the $100+ target cluster; if Waymo's direct-app move in Jan 2028 is followed by exclusivity losses elsewhere, the aggregator premium goes with it.

Delivery Hero acquisition

The largest deal in Uber's history, announced 16 Jul 2026 after months of stake-building. Terms and structure

  • Perimeter: Uber keeps DH businesses in 50 markets ($42.5B 2025 GB); SSW Partners buys 14 overlapping markets (Foodora Sweden/Norway/Austria/Czechia, Yemeksepeti et al.) for ~$1.6B, conditional on the Uber offer completing. Uber gets no control over the SSW assets.
  • Financing: balance-sheet cash plus new debt; committed ~€14B bridge. Gross leverage to stay <2×; investment-grade rating and buyback framework "unchanged."
  • Accretion: non-GAAP EPS accretive at close; high-single-digit % accretion by year three; ≥$1.2B annual synergy target (UBS: achievable, possibly conservative).
  • Commitments: Berlin HQ and headcount protected to 2029; €2B invested in Germany over five years; no domination/profit-transfer agreement for three years.
  • Approvals: BaFin offer document, merger control and financial regulatory clearances — regulatory risk is the main risk flagged by UBS.

SWOT

Synthesis of the sections above

Strengths S

  • Scale flywheel: 208M MAPCs, 3.9B trips/quarter, 300M trips/week — liquidity no AV developer can replicate quickly.
  • Cash machine: TTM FCF $10.1B, adj. EBITDA margin expanding 40 bp/yr on GB; capex ~0.6% of revenue.
  • Cross-platform: only 20% of users are on both Rides and Eats; that cohort grows 1.5× faster — a long runway inside the existing base.
  • Advertising: Delivery ad revenue +$362M y/y in Q2, near-100% incremental margin.
  • Growth outside the core: sparse markets, Moto, Teens, Women Preferences, Uber Black — growing faster than dense metros with healthy margins.

Weaknesses W

  • No owned autonomy IP: Uber's AV position is contractual, not technological; partners can and do leave (Waymo Phoenix).
  • Optical revenue drag: business-model changes cost ~8 pts of reported revenue growth, muddying the P&L for another year.
  • GAAP noise: equity-stake revaluations and tax valuation releases make GAAP EPS unusable; the market discounts the headline P/E.
  • Take-rate pressure by design: Moto/Brazil and low-cost products dilute Mobility take rate (~100 bp beyond the UK effect).
  • Capital creep: $10B+ AV commitments plus a $14.8B acquisition move the model away from asset-light.

Opportunities O

  • 15 AV cities by YE26 → "many more" in 2027: each launch is evidence for the aggregator thesis and a re-rating catalyst.
  • Delivery Hero: 99 markets, $236B pro-forma GB, ≥$1.2B synergies, Uber One and ads exported to Korea, the Gulf and LatAm.
  • Buyback resumption: 50%-of-FCF policy implies ~$5B/yr at current cash generation once the DH pivot is digested.
  • Hybrid-network regulation: if NJ/NY/DC adopt staggered rollouts, the operator with human supply wins the transition years.
  • Third-party AV financing: shifting fleet capex to sponsors would restore the asset-light narrative.

Threats T

  • Waymo direct in 2028 and Tesla's Cybercab at half of Waymo's price — the two largest fleets outside Uber's network.
  • Regulatory backlash: "regulatory capture" framing of Uber's driver-quota lobbying could slow its own AV launches.
  • Counterparty risk: Lucid's reset, Avride's NHTSA probe, Chinese partners (WeRide, Pony, Baidu) facing Western restrictions.
  • Deal risk: DH merger control across the EU, Korea and the Gulf; €14B bridge termed out in a higher-rate tape.
  • Labor and insurance: driver-classification litigation and US insurance costs (a 2026 tailwind that can reverse).
